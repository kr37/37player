Cool stuff

### Safety

- **No editing on 'Playing'** — the 'Playing' tab never lets you change a playlist, so an operator who's unsure or unpracticed can't accidentally break anything just by using it.
- **PIN lockout** — set a PIN in Settings and both 'Edit Playlists' and 'Library' will ask for it before they open, if you want to keep them out of reach.
- **10-minute revert** — leave 'Edit Playlists' or 'Library' untouched for 10 minutes and the app quietly switches the display back to 'Playing' on its own — it doesn't start any playback, just brings that tab back to the front — so whoever finds it next isn't looking at an editing screen they didn't expect.

### Handy features

- **Look ahead without losing your place** — while something is playing, clicking another playlist opens it over the playing one, which stays live underneath with its waveform in view. Pick a track, see its waveform, and click in it to choose where to start; then the play button (or Enter, from the top of the track) switches to it. Click its title to make it the active playlist, ready at its first track without playing (you're asked first if something is playing), or double-click the title to start it straight away. Escape, ✕, or a click outside closes it with nothing changed.
- **Waveform seekbar** — the waveform on the 'Playing' screen shows the shape of the verses and silences in the track, so you can click straight to the right spot when you need to.
- **Even loudness** — recordings from different sources can be much louder or softer than each other. Settings → Playback can even them out as they play, track by track or album by album (an album moves as a whole, so its own quiet and loud parts stay as recorded), and any single track can be turned up or down in its details (✎). Every player in the app follows the same levels, and the files themselves are never changed. To see each track's measured loudness, turn on 'Show loudness in Library' in the display settings (◐).
- **Stop** — put a Stop wherever you need one in a playlist and give it a name, like "Stop for meditation." Playback pauses there and waits for a click to continue — no more prayers starting up while someone's teaching.
- **Split track** — no need for Audacity. On the 'Library' tab, play a track to the point you want, click Split track, and edit the details for each half before saving them as two separate tracks.
- **Library cleanup** — 'Find duplicates', on the 'Library' tab, goes through copies of the same recording one group at a time (the kind left by importing the same CDs more than once), with each copy's artwork and details side by side, the albums they come from below, and a player to compare them. Mark each copy Keep, Delete or Ignore; take a better name or artwork from a copy before it goes; and playlists that used a deleted copy switch to the one you keep. Settings → Library cleanup also lists tracks whose audio is missing and files no browser can play, for review before anything is removed.
- **Tags that stay with the files** — change a track's name, artist, album, track number or artwork, and the audio file itself is updated to match, so an exported copy or another music player shows the same thing. The audio is never re-encoded for this, and every other tag the file holds (year, genre, comments, lyrics…) is kept. Works for MP3, M4A, FLAC, Opus, Ogg, WAV and AIFF, and for WebM without artwork. When Standardize makes a file smaller, it copies all of the original's tags onto the new file. Portable exports also put a cover.jpg in each album folder. Importing a folder works the other way: its picture (cover.jpg, folder.jpg and the like, or the folder's only picture, or one in the folder above it) goes to any track that has none of its own, and into the track's file too.
- **Printable playlists** — in 'Edit Playlists', the print button on a playlist (or on a folder, for every playlist in it) makes a page for planning a session with the teacher: each prayer's title and length in large type, and at every stop the time played so far, with the time still to come on the line after it. Print it, or save it as a PDF from the print dialog.
- **Side-by-side playlist editing** — the 'Edit Playlists' screen's two-pane layout makes it easy to build a playlist while browsing your library right alongside it.
- **Export** — two different formats, depending on what you need. One is a .zip meant purely for re-importing into 37 Player. The other unpacks into a plain folder structure any other player can use (volume adjustments and evening out stay behind in 37 Player — they aren't written into the files as ReplayGain tags):

```
music/artist/album/files
playlists/(your own folder structure)/playlist.m3u8
```
