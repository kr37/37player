Cool stuff

### Safety

- **No editing on 'Playing'** — the 'Playing' tab never lets you change a playlist, so an operator who's unsure or unpracticed can't accidentally break anything just by using it.
- **PIN lockout** — set a PIN in Settings and both 'Edit Playlists' and 'Library' will ask for it before they open, if you want to keep them out of reach.
- **Undo and redo** — on 'Edit Playlists' and 'Library', ↶ and ↷ beside Settings (or Ctrl/⌘-Z) take back and redo your last changes, even after reopening 37 Player; a shared library also keeps a History of every version from the last 30 days. See 'History and Undo'.
- **10-minute revert** — leave 'Edit Playlists' or 'Library' untouched for 10 minutes and the app quietly switches the display back to 'Playing' on its own — it doesn't start any playback, just brings that tab back to the front — so whoever finds it next isn't looking at an editing screen they didn't expect.

### Handy features

- **Look ahead without losing your place** — while something is playing, clicking another playlist opens it over the playing one, which stays live underneath with its waveform in view. Pick a track, see its waveform, and click in it to choose where to start; then the play button (or Enter, from the top of the track) switches to it. Click its title to make it the active playlist, ready at its first track without playing (you're asked first if something is playing), or double-click the title to start it straight away. Escape, ✕, or a click outside closes it with nothing changed.
- **Waveform seekbar** — the waveform on the 'Playing' screen shows the shape of the verses and silences in the track, so you can click straight to the right spot when you need to.
- **Even loudness** — recordings from different sources can be much louder or softer than each other. **Even out**, in the bar along the top of 'Playing', evens them out as they play, and Settings → Playback chooses how: track by track or album by album (an album moves as a whole, so its own quiet and loud parts stay as recorded), and any single track can be turned up or down in its details (✎). Evening out (and turning a track up yourself) can go past a quiet track's loudest moment, which helps on small speakers; its loudest moments then start to distort, and the track's details say by how much, so you can turn it down a touch. Every player in the app follows the same levels, and the files themselves are never changed. To see each track's measured loudness, turn on **Loudness** in the bar along the top of 'Library'.
- **Stop** — put a Stop wherever you need one in a playlist and give it a name, like "Stop for meditation." Playback pauses there and waits for a click to continue — no more prayers starting up while someone's teaching.
- **Browse's bars** — in 'Library' → Browse, each column has a heading and its own bar: Artists and Albums have **Edit** and **Delete** for those selected (select several albums and Edit gives them one name, merging them; the same for artists), and Tracks has the count and length of what's selected, Undo, Redo, Edit, Split, Delete, and **Player** and **Loudness**. The bar along the top chooses what's shown: Browse or Table, search, Show, and Duplicates.
- **Library as a table** — on the 'Library' tab, **Table** (beside **Browse**) shows every track in one list with details the browser leaves out: loudness, how many playlists use it, and (under **Columns**) type, size, bit rate, sample rate and channels, and, in a shared library, whether its audio is on this device. Click any heading to sort by it (again to reverse): Size, largest first, finds the giant files; **Changed**, newest first, finds what was just split, converted or renamed. The search box and **Show** above filter it (in Browse too): **Last import**, **Missing artist, album or artwork**, **Can't be played**, **Missing audio**, **Damaged files**, **Kept originals** (each track's current file beside the original it was converted from: under the name in Browse; in Table, what changed shows as c: over o:), and in a shared library **Not on this device**, each with its count; Library cleanup's lists have **Show in Table** to get there. In the bar below, **Columns** chooses what shows, **Wrap** shows long names in full, **Waveforms** puts each track's waveform behind its name, and **Fit**, **Scroll** or **Full** chooses how wide the columns are. Select tracks that can't be played and **Convert** appears beside the count of what's selected; a track's details (✎) offer it too. Selecting, ✎, Delete, Enter, Space and Export work as they do in Browse. On a narrow screen, swipe the table sideways; the name stays in view. It remembers the view, the sort and the columns on this device.
- **Splitting a recording** — no need for Audacity. Open a track's details (✎) and choose **Split…**, or **Split** in the bar along the top of 'Library' or 'Edit Playlists'. The splitter shows the whole track, and below it a close-up with a detailed waveform. Click to put the cursor where a cut should go, then move it a few milliseconds at a time with the arrow keys or the −10 ms / +10 ms buttons, so a cut can sit just before a breath; **Up to here** and **From here** play the two seconds either side of the cursor, at half speed if you like, or two or three times faster to find your place. **Mark here** (or <kbd>M</kbd>) cuts there; add as many marks as you need, and drag one to move it. Each piece has its own colour, on both waveforms and in the list below, is kept or left out, and has its own name, track number, artist and album. Marks dropped while listening go to the quietest moment nearby (**Find the quiet** does the same for a mark you've selected). Opened from a playlist in 'Edit Playlists', it asks at the end whether that entry should become the pieces; other playlists keep the original, which always stays in the library, and one Undo takes the whole split back. The pieces keep the original's loudness and volume, so they sound as it did, and each gets a waveform of its own. Nothing is encoded again unless your File format settings would have converted the original: WAV and MP3 are cut where they stand, at any length, even on a phone or iPad (with a 5 ms fade at a WAV's cut, and the one MP3 frame either side of the cut silenced, so it can't click); other formats are cut by the converter without re-encoding their sound; and a file the settings would convert (a WAV under "Make big files smaller", say) becomes pieces in the preferred format straight away.
- **Library cleanup** — **Duplicates**, in the bar along the top of the 'Library' tab, goes through copies of the same recording one group at a time (the kind left by importing the same CDs more than once), with each copy's artwork and details side by side, the albums they come from below, and a player to compare them. Mark each copy Keep, Delete or Ignore; take a better name or artwork from a copy before it goes; and playlists that used a deleted copy switch to the one you keep. Settings → Library cleanup starts with **Check library**, which looks at every track as an import would today and fixes what it can (it never changes names, artists, albums, artwork or track numbers); it also lists tracks whose audio is missing and tracks that can't be played. **Convert** turns a format a browser can't play (Windows Media Audio, Apple Lossless, compressed AIFF) into an MP3 that plays everywhere, keeping the track in its playlists; **Check again** looks at each stored file afresh and repairs what it can (an AIFF an older version left at 0:00 comes back as WAV); nothing is removed without your say.
- **Tags that stay with the files** — change a track's name, artist, album, track number or artwork, and the audio file itself is updated to match, so an exported copy or another music player shows the same thing. The audio is never re-encoded for this, and every other tag the file holds (year, genre, comments, lyrics…) is kept. Works for MP3, M4A, FLAC, Opus, Ogg, WAV and AIFF, and for WebM without artwork. When Standardize makes a file smaller, it copies all of the original's tags onto the new file. Portable exports also put a cover.jpg in each album folder. Importing a folder works the other way: its picture (cover.jpg, folder.jpg and the like, or the folder's only picture, or one in the folder above it) goes to any track that has none of its own, and into the track's file too.
- **Printable playlists** — in 'Edit Playlists', the print button on a playlist (or on a folder, for every playlist in it) makes a page for planning a session with the teacher: each prayer's title and length in large type, and at every stop the time played so far, with the time still to come on the line after it. Print it, or save it as a PDF from the print dialog.
- **Side-by-side playlist editing** — the 'Edit Playlists' screen's two-pane layout makes it easy to build a playlist while browsing your library right alongside it.
- **Export** — two different formats, depending on what you need. One is a .zip meant purely for re-importing into 37 Player. The other unpacks into a plain folder structure any other player can use (volume adjustments and evening out stay behind in 37 Player — they aren't written into the files as ReplayGain tags):

```
music/artist/album/files
playlists/(your own folder structure)/playlist.m3u8
```
