37 Player: Privacy

37 Player is a music player and playlist organizer. It runs in your web browser, or as a desktop app for Mac, Windows and Linux. It has no accounts, no advertising and no analytics, and sells nothing to anyone. It sets one cookie, and only if you turn on Automatic restore (below). This page says where your information goes when you use it.

Your library stays on your device

Your tracks, playlists and settings are kept on your own device: in the browser's storage for this website, or, in the desktop app, in folders you choose. They aren't sent anywhere unless you use one of the things below.

This website

37player.com serves the app itself. Like most web servers, it keeps ordinary access logs (the address a request came from, the time, and the page asked for) for a limited time, to keep the site running. They aren't used for anything else.

The desktop app is downloaded from GitHub, where 37 Player's code is kept, and this site's home page asks GitHub which version is the latest. GitHub sees those requests, as it would any visit; see GitHub's privacy statement.

Automatic restore (a cookie)

A browser can erase a website's storage, and 37 Player's library with it: Safari after 7 days without a visit, any browser when the disk is nearly full. If you turn on Automatic restore in Settings → Backups, 37player.com keeps what it would need to bring each library on that device back: the library's name, the browser, its shared library (the join code, or which Google Drive folder, and the device's name) and where its backups are (a folder's name, or the last backup file's name and date).

  • It's kept in one cookie in your browser, sealed with a key only the server has, so it means nothing to anyone else. The app's own pages can't read it, and it's sent to only one small script on 37player.com.
  • Nothing of it is stored on the server.
  • It lasts up to 400 days. Turning Automatic restore off removes it, and so does clearing this site's data in the browser.
  • It's off unless you turn it on, and it's only ever used to offer your library back.

Shared libraries on 37player.com

If you join or start a shared library kept on 37player.com, that library's tracks and playlists and the names of the devices in it are stored on this server, so that the devices sharing it can keep each other up to date. Anyone with the library's join code can open it.

  • Earlier versions of the library are kept for 30 days, so that a change can be undone (History).
  • Each device also sends the library its playback and sync logs (what it played and when, and what it sent and received), so that the people who look after the library can see what each device did. Each device's latest logs replace its last ones; logs not updated in 90 days are removed.
  • Leaving the library on your device doesn't remove it from the server. To have a shared library removed, contact us (below).

Shared libraries on Google Drive

If you choose to keep a shared library in Google Drive, 37 Player asks Google for permission to see, edit, create and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app (Google's drive.file access). With it, 37 Player can see only the folder and files it made itself. It can't see anything else in your Drive.

  • Your library's tracks, playlists, earlier versions and the devices' logs go directly between your device and your Google Drive. They never pass through 37player.com.
  • Signing in passes once through a small helper on 37player.com, which trades Google's one-time sign-in code for the keys your device needs to stay signed in. The helper stores nothing and logs nothing. The keys are kept only on your device. (The desktop app signs in through your web browser, the same way.)
  • 37 Player uses this access only to keep your shared library in sync. It doesn't share your Google data with anyone, use it for advertising, or use it for anything else.
  • You can remove 37 Player's access at any time in your Google Account, at myaccount.google.com/permissions. The library's folder stays in your Drive until you delete it.

37 Player's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Sending feedback

Send feedback (in Help, and in Settings → About) sends nothing until you press Send. Then it sends your message; your email address, if you give one for a reply; 37 Player's version, the browser, and the names of the device and library; and the logs you chose to include (the same kind of logs as above, which list track names and times).

  • It's emailed to the developer, kept on 37player.com, and filed in a private repository on GitHub, so that nothing is lost. To help find the cause of a problem, reports may be read with the help of an AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude).
  • Your email address is used only to reply to you.
  • To stop the form being misused, the server counts messages from each internet address, which it keeps only in a scrambled form (a hash).
  • To have a report you sent deleted, say so in a new message.

The desktop app

The desktop app keeps your libraries in folders on your computer. Once a day it asks GitHub whether there's a newer version of 37 Player; anything it downloads is signed, and is checked before it's used. Otherwise it sends nothing beyond what this page describes.

Converting audio

When 37 Player converts a file to another format, it does so on your device, with a converter downloaded once from 37player.com. Your audio isn't uploaded to be converted.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or a request to remove a shared library or a report: use Send feedback in 37 Player (it reaches only the developer), or github.com/kr37/37player/issues (public).
